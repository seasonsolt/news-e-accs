Answer-first summary rules:
1. Start with a self-contained sentence naming who did what and the central change or result. Answer the original headline; do not elevate a secondary demo into the main claim.
2. Use natural US English, normally 40–100 words and at most 3 compact sentences. Sparse sources may be shorter; do not pad them. Short X posts preserve the author's wording and line breaks when that wording is already English; render any other language in English.
3. Add only 1–2 supported details, prioritizing verifiable numbers, availability and implications explicitly given in the source. Preserve names, versions, units, attribution, links and uncertainty. Identify USD only if supported; do not convert currencies.
4. Avoid stock openings such as "This article discusses" and never invent an impact claim.

When the source introduces or demonstrates a previously released product, describe this introduction or demonstration as the current action and keep the earlier release as background.
